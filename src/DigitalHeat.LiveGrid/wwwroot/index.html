How to read it

Each panel is a house with three servers (three tasks can run at once). The slider sets the room temperature the house wants (an illustrative input, not a sensor); higher means more heat wanted this hour, and the room colour follows it. Whenever something changes (a job arrives, a server becomes free, a slider moves) and at least once per hour (2 s here), the network scheduler sets each server's load from that heat and moves waiting tasks whose house cannot finish them before their deadline.

Circles in the middle: the tasks running now; it fills as the real computation progresses, faster at higher load (ring = load the scheduler set; the measured CPU load is shown below it; pale = paused). A curved line shows a task moving between houses. Small circles: waiting tasks. Number = job, colour = house the job came from. 1 task ≈ 1 server-hour of real Ising Monte Carlo in a Docker container.

Keys: 1–4 new job in that house · S switch scheduler · A auto-spawn · R reset · ? this help
