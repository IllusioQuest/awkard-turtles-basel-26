\section{Demonstrator and measured jobs}
\label{sec:demonstrator}

The simulation and the laptop demonstration share one control boundary:
\texttt{IScheduler.Decide(HeatSignal)} chooses a target load and
\texttt{SchedulerController.Apply} sends it to an \texttt{IExecutor}. The simulated executor
settles the heat balance; \texttt{DockerJobExecutor} freezes, resumes or throttles a running
container. A successful freeze and resume demonstrates control of a process, not thermal recovery
from the laptop.

\paragraph{Live request.} In the pitch demonstrator a spoken or typed request becomes one container
that transcribes the request with Whisper large-v3 (whisper.cpp) and answers it with Apertus~8B
(llama.cpp), both offline on the laptop. One mesh peer (\texttt{MeshPeer}) admits, freezes and thaws
it with the policy of Section~\ref{sec:policy}. The house is a replayed 15~July 2025 at ten seconds per
simulated hour, the hot-water draws follow the EU standard tapping profile~L scaled to the case's
\qty{12}{kWh} per day \cite{eu814}, and the house's other servers run heat-first background work
that yields to the request. In the hour from 13:00, which has no draw, the tank is full and the
useful heat is zero, so the scheduler freezes the container (\texttt{docker pause}). Draws are
binned by hour as in the case model; the next hour (14:00--15:00) contains a draw, so the container
is thawed at 14:00. An independent probe, which read the
container's kernel counters every \qty{0.5}{s}, confirmed that the freeze stops the computation
itself (Table~\ref{tab:freeze}). The demonstrator also reports the container's CPU time from its
cgroup and its energy from the processor's package counter (RAPL), attributed by the container's
share of busy CPU time: one request used \qty{243}{s} of CPU time and about \qty{0.11}{Wh}. This is
the laptop's package energy, not that of a \qty{1}{kW} server.

\paragraph{Measured jobs.} The proposed Type~1 service is batch summarisation of non-personal documents with Apertus 8B.
On an eight-thread laptop CPU, a 14.98-minute transcript (3,310 input and 319 output tokens)
took a mean \qty{133.3}{s} in three runs. Its gross CPU-package energy was approximately
\qty{0.92}{Wh} per document; the battery meter was invalid with the charger connected
(measurement log in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). No L4 throughput or whole-machine power can be inferred
without an additional measurement.

The Type~2 representative is a checkpointable 2D Ising Monte Carlo parameter sweep. On a
separate laptop, a steady-state run completed 320 tasks in \qty{274.4}{s}, consuming
\qty{1.3089}{Wh} of gross whole-laptop battery energy, or about \qty{0.00409}{Wh} per task.
The battery meter updates slowly; a shorter run was discarded for this reason. Completed task
files are written atomically so a restart repeats only unfinished tasks. An earlier replay
(\texttt{LiveRun}) controlled this container with the same load rule (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
Both jobs establish executable workload types and interruption behaviour. Their laptop energy
figures cannot validate the abstract \qty{1}{kW} server, a liquid-cooled L4 build or a paying
customer workload.

A process-control trace is evidence of the common scheduler path; the annual heat results remain
simulated and should not be read as calorimetric measurements.
