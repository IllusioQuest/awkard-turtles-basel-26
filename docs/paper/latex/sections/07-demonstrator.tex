\section{Demonstrator and measured jobs}
\label{sec:demonstrator}

The annual run and the laptop demonstration use the same control boundary:
\texttt{IScheduler.Decide(HeatSignal)} chooses a target load and
\texttt{SchedulerController.Apply} sends it to an \texttt{IExecutor}. The simulated executor
settles the annual heat balance; \texttt{DockerJobExecutor} pauses, resumes or throttles a
running container. The network adapter commits an hourly decision through this path and rejects
a changed heat signal (decisions 0005, 0009 and 0024). A successful pause/resume demonstrates
control of a process, not thermal recovery from the laptop.

The proposed Type~1 service is batch summarisation of non-personal documents with Apertus 8B.
On an eight-thread laptop CPU, a 14.98-minute transcript (3,310 input and 319 output tokens)
took a mean \qty{133.3}{s} in three runs. Its gross CPU-package energy was approximately
\qty{0.92}{Wh} per document; the battery meter was invalid with the charger connected
(\texttt{docs/jobs/llm-summary.md}). No L4 throughput or whole-machine power can be inferred
without an additional measurement.

The Type~2 representative is a checkpointable 2D Ising Monte Carlo parameter sweep. On a
separate laptop, a steady-state run completed 320 tasks in \qty{274.4}{s}, consuming
\qty{1.3089}{Wh} of gross whole-laptop battery energy, or about \qty{0.00409}{Wh} per task.
The battery meter updates slowly; a shorter run was discarded for this reason. Completed task
files are written atomically so a restart repeats only unfinished tasks. The same heat scheduler
controlled this container in a \texttt{LiveRun} replay (\texttt{docs/jobs/research-sim.md}).
Both jobs establish executable workload types and interruption behaviour. Their laptop energy
figures cannot validate the abstract \qty{1}{kW} server, a liquid-cooled L4 build or a paying
customer workload.

The January and July replay commands and their deterministic output are documented in the
\texttt{DigitalHeat.LiveRun} project. A process-control trace is evidence of the common scheduler
path; the annual heat results remain simulated and should not be read as calorimetric measurements.
