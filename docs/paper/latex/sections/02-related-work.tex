\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Heat-reusing compute companies.} Several companies have sold computing whose heat warms
buildings. Qarnot started with room radiators and moved to ``digital boilers'' that feed hot water
and heating networks; its radiators were discontinued because of seasonality
\cite{qarnot_techcrunch2023}, and its HPC activities were acquired by Scaleway in 2026
\cite{qarnot_scaleway2026}. Heata places a server at a domestic hot-water cylinder and has run a
trial with British Gas \cite{heata_centrica2025}. Deep Green heats public swimming pools with
data-centre heat \cite{deepgreen_exmouth2023,deepgreen_register2024}. Leafcloud places servers in
buildings with a year-round hot-water demand \cite{leafcloud_heatreuse}. Cloud\&Heat began with
servers in homes and moved to data centres \cite{cloudandheat_history}; Nerdalize, which heated
Dutch homes with servers, went bankrupt \cite{nerdalize_bankruptcy}. Our reading of these cases
(\texttt{docs/research/competitors.md}, from press snippets, confidence per row noted there): the
residential failures sized for the winter peak, ran always on and lacked demand.

\paragraph{Swiss projects.} In Basel, IWB ran a pilot with Neutral-IT in which a mini data centre
in an oil bath heats a rental building with six flats, with gas kept as backup
\cite{iwb_neutralit}. IWB and DeepSquare reported a decentralised-cloud proof of concept with
immersion cooling \cite{deepsquare_epfl}. Infomaniak's D4 data centre in Geneva feeds district
heating via heat pumps \cite{infomaniak_d4}, and EPFL's Heating Bits project studies data-centre
heat for campus district heating with second-life servers \cite{epfl_heatingbits}
(\texttt{docs/research/digitalheat-company.md}~\S4).
